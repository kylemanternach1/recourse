Question Bank 09 - Enforcement if the User Wins

AI instruction
Use this bank only if the user won a money judgment or is trying to collect an existing small claims judgment. Ask only questions needed to determine whether enforcement can begin and which collection path is practical.

Stage goal
Track payment, waiting period, debtor assets, and collection options.

Model knowledge to apply when interpreting answers
- A judgment is a legal right to collect; it is not automatic payment.
- The user usually must wait through the appeal/enforcement waiting period before using involuntary collection tools.
- The defendant may pay voluntarily, request payments, appeal, ignore the judgment, file bankruptcy, or claim exemptions.
- The AI should maintain a payment ledger and update the remaining balance after every payment.
- Collection strategy depends on what the user knows about the debtor's job, bank, business, property, or assets.
- Writs, wage garnishment, bank levy, debtor examinations, and property liens are different tools with different forms and county procedures.
- Enforcement costs may be recoverable, but the user should understand that collection can take time and money.
- If the judgment is paid, the workflow must shift to satisfaction of judgment rather than more collection.

Always extract first if available
- judgment amount
- notice of entry date
- payment status
- debtor asset information
- appeal status
- enforcement method preference

High-priority questions
- Did you win a money judgment?
- What amount did the court award?
- What date was the Notice of Entry of Judgment mailed or received?
- Has the defendant paid anything?
- Has the 30-day appeal or waiting period passed?
- Did the defendant appeal?
- Did the defendant provide a Judgment Debtor's Statement of Assets, SC-133?
- Do you know where the defendant works?
- Do you know where the defendant banks?
- Does the defendant own real property?
- Has the defendant filed bankruptcy or claimed they cannot pay?

Waiting-state questions
- Are you still within the 30-day appeal or waiting period?
- Are you waiting for the defendant to make voluntary payment?
- Are you waiting for an installment payment?
- Are you waiting for SC-133 Judgment Debtor's Statement of Assets?
- Are you waiting for the sheriff, levying officer, employer, or bank to process an enforcement request?
- Are you waiting for the court to schedule a debtor examination?

Conditional questions
Ask if waiting period has not passed:
- Do you want to prepare a voluntary payment request while waiting?
- What date should enforcement options be revisited?

Ask if partial payments were made:
- What amount was paid and on what date?
- Was there a written payment agreement?
- What balance remains?

Ask if SC-133 was not provided:
- Do you want to consider a debtor examination?
- Do you have a current address where the debtor can be served?

Ask if wage garnishment is possible:
- What is the debtor's employer name and address?
- Is the debtor an employee rather than self-employed?

Ask if bank levy is possible:
- Do you know the bank or branch where the debtor holds funds?
- Do you have enough identifying information for the levy?

Ask if real property lien is possible:
- What county is the property in?
- Do you want to record an abstract of judgment?

Ask if judgment is fully paid:
- Do you need to file an Acknowledgment of Satisfaction of Judgment?
- Were any liens, levies, or garnishments started that need release?

Ask if bankruptcy is mentioned:
- What bankruptcy court notice did you receive?
- Has collection been stayed?

Do not ask
- Do not ask appeal-prep questions unless an appeal has been filed.
- Do not ask new case intake questions unless enforcing this judgment is impossible.

Validation logic
- Enforcement usually begins after the appeal/waiting period.
- Payments reduce the collectible balance.
- Collection tools require accurate debtor information.
- Writs, garnishments, levies, liens, and debtor exams use different forms.
- Bankruptcy may pause collection.
- Satisfaction must be filed when judgment is paid.

Risk flags
- enforcement_too_early
- appeal_pending
- debtor_assets_unknown
- bankruptcy_flag
- payment_ledger_missing
- satisfaction_required
- collection_costs_may_exceed_recovery

Response behavior
- If enforcement is too early, give the date when the user should revisit enforcement.
- If payment is partial or promised, update the ledger and set the next payment checkpoint.
- If the debtor ignores the judgment, recommend the least complex viable enforcement path based on available asset information.
- If asset information is missing, ask only the questions needed to pick between debtor exam, wage garnishment, bank levy, or lien.
- If bankruptcy is mentioned, tell the user collection may be stayed and mark the case for legal review.

Data fields to populate
- judgment_amount
- judgment_date
- sc_130_mailing_date
- enforceable_date
- appeal_status
- payments_received
- remaining_balance
- debtor_asset_info
- sc_133_status

- writ_status

Stage completion standard
Stop asking enforcement questions when the AI can recommend a practical collection path, explain what information is missing, or confirm that collection should pause or close.
