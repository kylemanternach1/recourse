Question Bank 06 - Service of Process and Proof of Service

AI instruction
Use this bank after the user has a file-stamped claim. Ask only questions needed to choose a valid service method, track service attempts, and complete proof of service.

Stage goal
Ensure every defendant is properly served and proof of service is filed before the deadline.

Model knowledge to apply when interpreting answers
- Service gives the court power to proceed against the defendant. Without valid service, the court generally cannot enter judgment against that defendant.
- The plaintiff cannot personally serve the papers. Service must be done by a qualified adult non-party or an authorized service provider.
- The served papers should usually include the file-stamped SC-100 and related filed documents.
- Service deadlines are tied to the hearing date and defendant location.
- Service on a business is different from service on an individual. Corporations and LLCs usually require service on a registered agent or authorized person.
- Government defendants may require special service rules.
- Proof of service is not complete just because service happened. The proof form must be completed correctly and filed or brought according to court rules.
- If service fails, the AI should help plan another attempt, check whether there is enough time, or explain that the user may need a continuance.
- After service, there may be a waiting period before the hearing. The AI should shift the user to monitoring proof acceptance, possible defendant response/counterclaim, and hearing prep.

Always extract first if available
- defendant type
- service address
- registered agent
- hearing date
- service deadline
- service method
- proof of service status

High-priority questions
- Do you have a file-stamped copy of SC-100 to serve?
- Is each defendant located in the same county as the court, another California county, another state, or unknown?
- What address should be used to serve each defendant?
- What type of defendant is this: individual, corporation, LLC, partnership, sole proprietor, government entity, or unknown business?
- Who will serve the papers?
- Has each defendant been successfully served?
- What date, time, place, and method of service were used?
- Has the proof of service been filed with the court?

Waiting-state questions
- Are you waiting for the sheriff, process server, or other server to report whether service succeeded?
- Are you waiting for the court to process the proof of service?
- Are you waiting to see whether the defendant contacts you, pays, settles, or files a counterclaim?
- Has the court posted any update about service or hearing status?

Conditional questions
Ask if defendant is an individual:
- Is this the defendant's home, work, or another reliable address?
- Is personal service possible?

Ask if defendant is a corporation or LLC:
- Who is the registered agent for service of process?
- Is the agent's address current?

Ask if defendant is a government entity:
- Which office, clerk, secretary, governing body, or official must be served?
- Have you verified the government service requirements?

Ask if using a friend or adult non-party:
- Is the server at least 18?
- Is the server not a plaintiff, defendant, or otherwise part of the case?
- Can the server complete and sign the proof of service?

Ask if using sheriff, process server, or clerk service:
- Which agency or provider will perform service?
- What fee is charged?
- Do they need special instructions or copies?

Ask if service failed:
- What happened during the service attempt?
- Is there another address?
- Is there another authorized person who can be served?
- Is there enough time to attempt service again before the deadline?

Ask after service:
- Did the server complete SC-104?
- Does the proof list the correct defendant, date, time, place, and service method?
- How will the proof be filed with the court?

Do not ask
- Do not ask for evidence or hearing argument unless service is complete or the hearing is near.
- Do not ask post-judgment questions here.

Validation logic
- Plaintiff cannot personally serve the defendant.
- Server must be an adult non-party.
- Service deadlines depend on whether the defendant is in the filing county or outside it.
- Corporations and LLCs should be served through registered agent or authorized person.
- Government defendants may require special service.
- Judgment may be delayed, continued, or impossible without valid proof of service.

- service_address_uncertain
- registered_agent_missing

Response behavior
- If service is not started, recommend a valid service method based on defendant type and deadline.
- If service is pending, explain what confirmation is needed and when to check status.
- If service failed, ask for the failure reason and suggest the next procedural option.
- If service succeeded, move immediately to proof of service filing and then hearing preparation.
- If the defendant responds informally, distinguish between settlement/payment and formal court filings such as SC-120.

Stage completion standard
Stop asking service questions when every defendant has been served and proof of service has been filed or is ready to file before the deadline.
