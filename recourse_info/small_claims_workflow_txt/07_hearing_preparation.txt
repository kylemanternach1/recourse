Question Bank 07 - Hearing Preparation

AI instruction
Use this bank once the hearing date is known and service is complete or in progress. Ask only questions needed to prepare exhibits, witnesses, script, defenses, counterclaims, and settlement options.

Stage goal
Create a clear hearing packet and presentation plan.

Model knowledge to apply when interpreting answers
- Small claims hearings are informal compared with regular civil trials, but the user still needs proof, organization, and a clear request.
- The judge may have limited time. The user's presentation should be chronological, concise, and tied to documents.
- The user should be prepared for three outcomes: judgment, continuance, or dismissal. Settlement or mediation may also happen before or at the hearing.
- The defendant may appear, fail to appear, dispute service, raise defenses, or file a Defendant's Claim/counterclaim.
- The AI should prepare the user to answer likely defenses rather than only presenting the plaintiff's story.
- County rules may control remote appearances, exhibit upload, exhibit exchange, or paper copies. These must be verified.
- After filing and service, the user may be waiting for the hearing, a defendant response, a counterclaim, settlement contact, or court instructions. The AI should track those waiting states and remind the user what to monitor.

Always extract first if available
- hearing date and mode
- proof of service status
- exhibit list
- witness list
- expected defenses
- counterclaim status
- settlement interest

High-priority questions
- Has each defendant been served?
- Has proof of service been filed?
- What are the three to five most important facts the judge needs to understand?
- Which exhibit proves each important fact?
- Do you have copies of each exhibit for the judge, yourself, and the defendant, or whatever your county requires?
- Are your texts, emails, photos, receipts, estimates, and invoices organized and readable?
- Do any witnesses need to attend?
- What defenses do you expect the defendant to raise?
- Is the hearing in person, remote, or hybrid?
- Has the defendant filed a Defendant's Claim, SC-120?

Waiting-state questions
- Are you waiting for the hearing date to arrive?
- Has the defendant contacted you to settle, pay, or dispute the claim?
- Have you received any new paperwork from the defendant or court?
- Has the court sent remote hearing instructions or exhibit instructions?
- Are you waiting on a witness, document, subpoena, or repair estimate?

Conditional questions
Ask if proof of service is not complete:
- What remains to complete service?
- Is there enough time before the hearing deadline?

Ask if witnesses are needed:
- What does each witness know from personal experience?
- Is any witness unwilling or unable to attend voluntarily?
- Do you need a subpoena for a witness or documents?

Ask if defendant filed a counterclaim:
- What is the defendant asking for?
- What facts and evidence respond to the defendant's claim?
- Are there documents that disprove or reduce the counterclaim?

Ask if remote hearing:
- Does the county require evidence upload before the hearing?
- Do you have a working device, camera, microphone, internet connection, and quiet location?
- Did the court provide a remote link or instructions?

Ask if settlement is possible:
- What amount would you accept to settle before the hearing?
- Would payment over time be acceptable?
- Do you want proposed settlement terms prepared in writing?

Ask if exhibits are messy:
- Should we organize exhibits chronologically or by issue?
- Which documents are duplicates, irrelevant, or confusing?

Do not ask
- Do not ask enforcement questions before judgment.
- Do not re-open eligibility unless a new fact creates a serious issue.

Validation logic
- Hearing script amount must match SC-100 and damages sheet.
- Evidence should be tied to key facts.
- Witnesses should have first-hand knowledge.
- Subpoenas require lead time.
- County exhibit and remote-hearing rules must be verified.
- Counterclaims require a separate response plan.

Risk flags
- service_not_complete
- exhibit_packet_incomplete
- evidence_not_readable
- witness_unavailable
- subpoena_needed
- county_exhibit_rule_unverified
- counterclaim_unprepared
- remote_hearing_logistics_missing

Response behavior
- If the user is simply waiting for the hearing, provide a preparation timeline rather than asking unnecessary intake questions.
- If defendant contacts the user, help classify it as settlement, informal dispute, payment, or formal court response.
- If a counterclaim arrives, switch to preparing both claim and defense.
- If new evidence appears, update the exhibit index and damages/hearing script as needed.
- If local hearing rules are unknown, prompt county-rule verification before final hearing packet.

Data fields to populate
- proof_of_service_ready
- hearing_mode
- exhibit_list
- exhibit_copy_status
- witness_attendance_status
- subpoena_needed
- expected_defenses
- counterclaim_status
- settlement_position
- hearing_script
- county_hearing_rules
- waiting_for_hearing_status
- defendant_contact_log
- court_notice_log

Stage completion standard
Stop asking hearing-prep questions when the user has an organized exhibit packet, witness plan, defense/counterclaim response, and concise hearing script.
